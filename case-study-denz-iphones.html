Case study / E-commerce

Denz iPhones storefront.

A real iPhone business needed a storefront that could show devices clearly, handle different stock conditions and make the path from browsing to ordering simple on a phone.

Visit live website ↗Back to work

The project

A product-heavy website needed to stay easy to use.

Denz iPhones sells different iPhone models with changing stock, storage sizes, colours and device conditions. That creates a different website problem from a simple brochure site: customers need to compare products quickly, understand what is available and move toward an order without opening unnecessary pages or getting lost in the catalogue.

Product UX

Browse without friction

Product cards were treated as useful shopping surfaces rather than simple links, with important choices kept close to the product.

Responsive

Designed around mobile use

The storefront was built to remain clear on smaller screens because many customers will browse and order from a phone.

Real inventory

Stock has to stay understandable

The project supports different product conditions, prices, storage options and availability instead of pretending every phone is the same.

What the business received

A storefront designed around how customers browse and buy.

Product presentation

The catalogue was organised so customers can compare models, prices, device details and options without feeling overwhelmed.

Categories and stock states

The store was structured around the business’s real categories so sold-out or changing stock is easier for customers to understand.

Colour and storage choices

Colour and storage choices stay close to the product so customers do not need to jump through unnecessary pages.

Cart and checkout UX

The shopping journey keeps customer details, product choices and the path to ordering clear.

Delivery and collection logic

The project includes the business rules needed to distinguish delivery from collection and present the relevant checkout information.

Live website

The finished website is live on a public domain so potential clients can check the real customer experience for themselves.

What was improved along the way

Good e-commerce depends on getting the details right.

The build involved repeated small corrections: product images, model labels, colour matching, category wording, storage options, logo placement, mobile behaviour and checkout details. Those changes matter because an online store can look polished while still confusing customers if the product data or buying flow is wrong. The project was refined around the actual stock information instead of leaving placeholder catalogue content in the final version.
